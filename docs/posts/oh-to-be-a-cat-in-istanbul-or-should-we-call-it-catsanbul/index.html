Oh, to Be a Cat in Istanbul (or Should We Call It Catsanbul?)

By govher_s · 15 August 2026

Oh, to be a cat in Istanbul. Or should we call it Catsanbul?

I came for the mosques, the bazaars and the Bosphorus. I left with a camera roll that is at least seventy percent cats. Nobody warned me, and I would like to file a complaint with every guidebook I read.

They run the place

The cats here are not strays in the way I understand the word. They do not belong to anyone, and they belong to everyone. They sleep on shop counters, on motorbike seats, on stacks of carpets worth more than my flight. Nobody moves them. You simply shop around the cat.

On my first morning I sat down on a bench near the bazaar and this white and tabby gentleman sat down next to me like we had an appointment. He did not want food. He did not want to be petted. He just wanted to sit there and judge the tourists with me. It was the best company I had all week.

Everyone looks after them

Once you start noticing, you see it everywhere. Little bowls of water outside doorways. Tiny wooden houses tucked beside the shops. A man at a tea stall breaking his simit in half without even looking down, because he knew exactly who was waiting at his feet.

How to be a good guest

Let them come to you. They will, and usually at the exact moment you sit down with something to eat.

Do not pick them up. You are in their city.

Leave room in your day. You are going to lose an hour to a cat on a wall, and it will be an hour well spent.

I have seen the big sights now, and they are as beautiful as everyone says. But when I think of Istanbul, I think of that one cat on that one bench, squinting into the sun like he owned the whole street. He probably does.
