Learning to Ski Somewhere in Norway

By Jeren_p · 5 September 2026

I had never put on a pair of skis before this trip. I would like that on the record before anyone reads any further.

Where exactly was I?

Honestly, somewhere in Norway is the best I can do. We drove north for hours, the road got whiter and whiter, and at some point there were no more towns, only spruce trees carrying so much snow that they looked like they were slouching. The cabins were all painted black with red window frames, and every roof had a thick white duvet on top.

Day one: gravity wins

My friends told me the beginner slope was basically flat. It was not flat. I fell getting off the lift, I fell standing still, and at one point I fell while taking my skis off. A child of about five went past me backwards.

The good news is that Norwegian snow is very soft, and nobody laughs at you. Everyone I crashed near simply asked if I was okay and then gave me one small piece of advice. Bend your knees. Look where you want to go. Stop looking at your feet.

Day three: something clicks

Somewhere around the third morning I made it all the way down without sitting on the mountain. It was slow and it was not pretty, but the sun was out, the sky was that sharp winter blue, and the only sound was my skis on the snow. I understood why people do this.

Tips from a total beginner

Take a lesson on the first day. I waited until the second, and I regret it.

Dress in layers. You will be freezing on the lift and sweating by the bottom.

Learn where the ski patrol hut is. I never needed it, but I walked past it every morning and gave it a respectful nod just in case.

Bring chocolate. There is a whole Norwegian tradition of eating it on the mountain, and it is the part of skiing I was good at immediately.

I am already looking at flights for next winter.
