They actually built this!

By ayna_matiyeva · 25 June 2026

There aren't many places where you can turn a corner and stumble across something that's been standing for nearly 2,000 years. In Rome, that's just part of the experience. I spent the day exploring the Roman Forum and the Colosseum, and honestly, the history is even cooler when you realize what the hell you're looking at.

**First stop: The Roman Forum**

Think of the Forum as ancient Rome's downtown. It was the center of political, religious, and public life, filled with temples, government buildings, and gathering spaces. Roman citizens came here to do business, hear speeches, and argue politics — some things apparently never change.

As Rome grew from a republic into an empire, its leaders filled the Forum with monuments celebrating military victories and powerful emperors. Today, much of it is ruins, but it's fascinating to imagine this place bustling with thousands of people going about their everyday lives.

**Next up: The Colosseum**

Built in the first century CE, the Colosseum could hold tens of thousands of spectators. Romans gathered here for public spectacles, including gladiator fights and animal hunts. Basically, they built a gigantic stadium for some seriously fucked-up entertainment.

The engineering is impressive, too. Its arches and carefully designed entrances helped move enormous crowds, while underground passages allowed performers and animals to enter the arena.

**So, what happened?**

The Western Roman Empire fell in 476 CE, but Rome continued evolving. Earthquakes, neglect, and centuries of reusing building materials left these famous structures in ruins.

Standing among them is a strange reminder that even the mightiest empires eventually fall apart.

And yet, here we are, thousands of years later, walking through their world and thinking, *Holy shit, they actually built this.*

History feels a lot different when you're standing right in the middle of it.
