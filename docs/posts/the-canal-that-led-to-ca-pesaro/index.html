The Canal That Led to Ca’ Pesaro

By ayna_matiyeva · 22 June 2026

I got lost on purpose.

Everyone tells you to put the map away in Venice and, for once, I listened. After twenty minutes of wandering through narrow passageways, crossing little stone bridges, and turning down streets that seemed to lead nowhere, I finally understood why.

Venice rewards the curious.

I had set out that morning with a loose plan to explore the canals, but somewhere between a tiny café and a shop selling hand-painted masks, I lost track of where I was supposed to be. The streets grew quieter, the crowds thinned, and the sounds of the city began to change. Footsteps echoed against old stone walls. Water lapped gently against the sides of buildings. Somewhere nearby, a boat engine hummed to life.

Then I found the canal.

It wasn't the grand, bustling stretch of water I'd seen in postcards. This one was narrower, tucked between weathered buildings with green shutters and laundry hanging from upper windows. The afternoon light shimmered across the water, breaking into a thousand little reflections every time a boat passed. I stood on the bridge for a moment, watching the city move at its own unhurried pace.

Eventually, I made my way toward Ca’ Pesaro, the grand Baroque palace that sits along the Grand Canal. From the water, its imposing stone façade felt almost theatrical, a reminder that Venice has spent centuries turning everyday life into something extraordinary. I could have hurried inside, checked off the landmark, and moved on to the next stop. Instead, I lingered along the canal, watching gondolas slip past water taxis and imagining how many people had traveled this same route long before me.

There is something strange about experiencing a city without constantly checking where you are. You notice the little things: the chipped paint around a doorway, the flowers spilling from a windowsill, the way sunlight catches the ripples beneath an old bridge. You begin to remember a place not by its street names, but by how it made you feel.

By the time I reached Ca’ Pesaro, I wasn't entirely sure how I'd gotten there. And somehow, that made arriving all the more satisfying.

Maybe getting lost isn't really about losing your way. Maybe it's about giving yourself permission to discover something you wouldn't have thought to look for.

Venice has plenty of famous views, and I'll probably remember the big landmarks for years. But I have a feeling I'll remember that quiet canal, too — the one I stumbled upon when I stopped trying to find my way.

Next time someone tells me to put the map away, I won't need convincing.
